About us

We don't do marketing. We just weave.

There isn't much to say about us, and we've never been good at talking about ourselves. Many of us started in 2014, when the war first came to the east of Ukraine. Others joined after February 2022, among them women who had to leave their homes in the east, moved to Kyiv and wanted to be useful.

Every one of us has a family, a job, a life of her own. Yet every day the frames fill up again. However tired she is, each volunteer finds the time to come in and keep weaving.

No big speeches. Just strips of fabric, tied one by one, by people who refuse to stop.

How we work

A workshop that never stops

Strip by strip, fabric is woven into a base mesh stretched across large frames. Finished nets are packed into bales and sent directly to units at the front.

Scale

From a single frame to a helicopter

Our nets have grown with the need. Today we weave them on a giant scale, big enough to cover trucks and heavy military vehicles. Once, we even made a net for a helicopter.

Why it matters

Why a net still matters in a war of drones

This war is watched from the sky. Reconnaissance drones hang over the front line day and night, and whatever they spot can be hit within minutes. A camouflage net breaks up the outline of a vehicle, a position or a piece of equipment, so it disappears into the field or forest around it.

01

What can't be seen can't be hit

Camouflage protects not only the metal, but the people inside the vehicle or the dugout.

02

Small cost, huge difference

A net costs a tiny fraction of the vehicle it hides. And nothing compares to the lives of the crew.

03

Always in demand

Nets get torn, burnt and shredded by shrapnel, and the landscape shifts from summer green to autumn brown. Units need replacements all the time.

Next to everything this war demands, a net may look like a small thing. For the crew underneath it, it isn't small at all. That's why we keep weaving.

Support us

Your donation becomes fabric

Our volunteers give their time and hands for free. What we can't make ourselves is the material, so every euro you donate goes to buying fabric for the nets.

Monthly minimum

€3,000

This is how much fabric our volunteers can weave through in a month: the minimum we have to raise to keep the workshop running at full capacity. Anything above it goes straight into more fabric and more nets.

There are no small donations

Our strength isn't a few big donors, but many people who care. €5 or €10 a month, given regularly, becomes a stable budget the workshop can rely on.
